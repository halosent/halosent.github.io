Research and further reading

Sources worth trusting.

Start with the Church's own teaching, then read the primary texts and serious scholarship behind it.

This collection prioritises official Orthodox jurisdictions, primary Christian texts, and established academic resources. No single website replaces the Bible, the liturgical life of the Church, the Ecumenical Councils, or guidance from a knowledgeable Orthodox priest. These links are a starting point for study, not a claim that every page has equal authority.

Reference and research

Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy

In-depth scholarly articles on metaphysics, ethics, philosophy of religion, and historical thinkers. Use it to understand arguments in their strongest form, including criticism.

Explore the encyclopedia ↗
How sources are ranked on Live Orthodox: for Orthodox doctrine, begin with Scripture as read within the Church, the Nicene Creed, the Ecumenical Councils, liturgical tradition, and recognised Orthodox teaching. For what a historical figure said, consult the primary text. For claims about history, use specialist scholarship and cite the edition or study. For philosophical arguments, represent both the argument and its strongest objections. An official church website is authoritative about its own teaching, but not automatically a neutral authority on every historical or scientific question.
